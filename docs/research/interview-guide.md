# Teoyube formative pilot moderator and interview guide

Status: **DRAFT - OWNER DECISION REQUIRED BEFORE USE**

## Moderator posture

Be neutral, curious, and non-defensive. Do not teach participants how to praise the product, correct their theology during usability tasks, imply that a recommendation is spiritually right, or request private disclosure. Safety intervention is the exception.

## Opening script

"We are studying the product, not testing you or your faith. Please use only the fictional scenarios we provide. You may skip, pause, or stop at any time. Think aloud by telling me what you notice, expect, and understand. I will not help immediately because confusion is useful product evidence."

Confirm consent, withdrawal rights, recording preference, and the no-private-content rule. If the optional live-AI task is approved, do not rely on general study consent; obtain the separate live-AI consent immediately before that task.

## Consistent task questions

Use these questions without leading toward a positive response:

- What do you think this section is telling you?
- Which words are Scripture, and which words are the app's explanation?
- Why do you think this recommendation appeared?
- How certain does the app seem?
- What would you do if you disagreed with this suggestion?
- What do you believe the app remembered?
- How would you remove or revoke that information?
- Did anything feel pressuring, manipulative, spiritually authoritative, or unclear?
- What would make this experience more trustworthy?

Additional neutral probes:

- What did you expect to happen?
- What changed after that action?
- Where would you look for the source or limitation?
- How would you reverse this decision?
- What is the difference between these two paths?

## Rescue protocol

1. Wait and observe.
2. Repeat the scenario exactly.
3. Ask a neutral orientation question.
4. Indicate a region without naming the control.
5. Name the control or explain the concept, recording a critical rescue.

Never rescue by asserting a theological conclusion, calling, promise fulfillment, or divine meaning.

## Safety stop

If a participant raises immediate danger, self-harm, abuse, dangerous divine commands, severe distress, a privacy incident, or another adverse event, stop the research task. Follow `adverse-event-protocol.md`, prioritize human support, avoid theological interpretation, and record only the minimum safe incident.

## Post-task debrief

Ask:

1. What felt clear or unclear?
2. Which source or explanation did you trust most, and why?
3. Did any distinction between Scripture and Teoyube content become confusing?
4. Did you feel free to disagree, reject, undo, or leave?
5. Did the product seem humble about calling and uncertainty?
6. Did memory and external processing feel under your control?
7. Which modules felt fragmented, redundant, or unnecessary?
8. Did the experience feel spiritually useful without feeling manipulative or judgmental?
9. What accessibility barrier, if any, had the greatest effect?
10. Is there non-sensitive feedback you want removed before analysis?

Remind participants how to request deletion and that benefits are not guaranteed. Do not promise product changes or interpret spiritual experiences.
