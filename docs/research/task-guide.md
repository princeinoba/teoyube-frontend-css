# Teoyube formative pilot task guide

Status: **DRAFT - SYNTHETIC SCENARIOS ONLY**

## Outcome definitions

- Success: the participant completes the intended outcome and explains the governing authority or control correctly without moderator rescue.
- Partial success: the participant completes the outcome with a noncritical misunderstanding, extra exploration, or one low-level prompt.
- Failure: the outcome is not completed or the participant retains a material misunderstanding after the allowed rescue.
- Moderator rescue: the moderator gives procedural guidance beyond rereading the neutral task. Record count and level.
- Critical misunderstanding: the participant treats interpretation as Scripture, AI as divine authority, calling as certain destiny, testimony/fulfillment as app-confirmed, memory as hidden or irreversible, or fallback as fabricated certainty.
- Safety stop: the adverse-event protocol is triggered; end the affected task and record only a minimal safe incident.

## Synthetic scenario set

Use neutral fictional profiles such as "Jordan is considering a volunteer teaching opportunity" or "Alex wants a Scripture passage about wisdom." Do not adapt scenarios from a participant's private life.

## Tasks

### Task 1 - Orientation

Ask the participant to explain what Teoyube appears to do; locate Today, Search, Promise Table, Calling Compass, and Teo Guide; and identify where sources and limitations appear.

Success requires an explanation centered on Scripture-supported formation rather than an AI oracle, plus correct location of the five areas and at least one source/limitation control.

### Task 2 - Exact Scripture

Retrieve the supplied reference `Ephesians 1:18`, verify the displayed translation/source, and identify exact verse text versus explanation.

Success requires the correct reference/source and no classification of Teoyube commentary as Scripture.

### Task 3 - Search and Promise Search

Use Search for the fictional concern "Jordan needs wisdom for a volunteer decision." Then use Promise Search for "promises about wisdom and direction." Explain the difference.

Success requires describing Search as broader intent discovery and Promise Search as promise-focused, without collapsing the two workflows.

### Task 4 - Promise Level

Inspect supplied results labeled A, B, and C. Explain direct promise, biblical principle, and personalized application.

Success requires correct authority ordering and recognition that Level C does not rewrite doctrine or guarantee an outcome.

### Task 5 - Prayer

Review a supplied Scripture-grounded prayer draft, identify Scripture versus devotional wording, edit one neutral sentence, then reject a second draft without saving private text.

Success requires correct distinction and successful edit/reject behavior.

### Task 6 - Calling

Review fictional calling indicators for Jordan. Explain why they are tentative, inspect supporting evidence and limitations, and name a wise-counsel next step.

Success requires no final-destiny interpretation and includes prayer, Scripture, trusted counsel, community, or appropriate qualified care.

### Task 7 - Daily action and reversibility

Accept a synthetic suggested action, edit it, reject another, undo, and revisit the prior step.

Success requires all transitions without fear that rejection implies spiritual failure or destroys progress.

### Task 8 - Reflection and testimony

Complete a fictional reflection flow, create a testimony candidate, and explain who confirms testimony or promise fulfillment.

Success requires identifying the user as the only confirmer and avoiding an automatic divine-action claim.

### Task 9 - Memory and consent

Inspect consent, identify what is remembered for the fictional profile, revoke one purpose, export the allowed record, delete it, and explain what changed.

Success requires purpose-specific understanding and successful inspection, revocation, export, and deletion.

### Task 10 - Deterministic Teo Guide

Ask the supplied synthetic spiritual-growth question, inspect sources, and explain uncertainty, limitations, and the distinction between Scripture and Teo Guide interpretation.

Success requires accurate mode and authority understanding.

### Task 11 - Live-AI preview

Only if owner-approved and separately consented, submit a synthetic non-sensitive question. Identify model use, exact sources, limitations, and any write-confirmation boundary; then revoke consent.

Success requires recognizing external processing, non-divine status, citation/source boundaries, and continued deterministic availability after revocation.

### Task 12 - Provider fallback

Use an approved simulated or provider-disabled state. Ask the participant what changed and whether the product remains useful.

Success requires understanding that deterministic Scripture, safety, TIG, and fallback remain available and that no unavailable provider result was fabricated.

### Task 13 - Hybrid retrieval

Use the supplied semantic query. Inspect why sources matched and explain what vector similarity means.

Success requires recognizing similarity as a ranking aid, not spiritual certainty, and exact Scripture as authoritative.

### Task 14 - Accessibility

Complete assigned steps by keyboard, zoom/reflow, reduced-motion preference, and screen reader or other assistive technology where applicable. Inspect focus, drawers/modals, media controls, errors, sources, consent, and deletion.

Success requires completing the assigned flow without a high-impact barrier. Record barriers using the accessibility guide.

### Task 15 - Complete journey

Using synthetic content, complete Today -> Check-in -> Scripture -> Promise -> Prayer -> Calling discernment -> Daily assignment -> Reflection -> Testimony candidate -> Book review -> Tomorrow. Explain what carries forward.

Success requires continuity, provenance, reversible transitions, correct user-confirmation boundaries, and no critical moderator rescue.

## Rescue hierarchy

1. Repeat the task verbatim.
2. Ask a neutral orientation question such as "What options do you see?"
3. Point to the relevant page region without naming the control.
4. Name the control or required concept; this is critical moderator rescue for threshold reporting.
5. Stop and follow the safety protocol when necessary.
